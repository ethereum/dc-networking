Staggered publishing vs subrounds

Two ways to schedule finality (FFG) votes and aggregates in a slot, and how each one shares the network with the block and the Goldfish votes.

Per node in 2 subnets · 12 s slot · 2,500 FFG voters per subnet per slot · effective gossipsub factor 5 · aggregate overhead 350 B

  • One mechanism, two settings. An aggregation point defines a committee. Staggered publishing has 1 aggregation point per subnet per slot. Subrounds have 4.
  • On average, staggered publishing is cheaper. Votes cost the same in both systems. Subrounds send 2.6× the aggregate bytes, because each aggregation point adds 16 × 64 aggregates with 350 B of fixed overhead. With all traffic: 7.8 Mbit/s against 11.4 Mbit/s per node.
  • At peak, subrounds can be better. Staggered publishing peaks at 54 Mbit/s at 9 s, when 1,024 aggregates of 663 B arrive together. This is more than the 50 Mbit/s download of a home node.
  • Subrounds aligned to the slot are the worst case. Each subround starts with a vote burst and an aggregate burst. If a subround starts at 0 s, both land on the block and the Goldfish votes: 94 Mbit/s.
  • Subrounds can move. Staggered publishing cannot. FFG votes do not use the block, so the subround grid is free. Shift it by 1 s and publish aggregates 1.5 s after the votes. The peak falls to 35 Mbit/s, and the slot start carries only the block and the Goldfish votes (34 Mbit/s).

Every byte counts 5 times: a node receives each message about 5 times (effective gossipsub factor). We assume that a node sends about as many bytes as it receives. A home node has 50 Mbit/s down and 25 Mbit/s up.

2. Staggered publishing

One committee per subnet votes in each slot. It votes in 7 groups, one group per second from 0 s to 6 s. At 9 s, the aggregators publish one aggregate per subnet for the whole committee. The aggregates spread until the next block at 12 s.

Good

  • Fewest aggregates. The fixed aggregate overhead is paid once per subnet per slot.
  • Long collection time. Group 1 has 9 s to reach the aggregators. The last group has 3 s.
  • Small vote bursts. Each group is 357 voters per subnet.

Bad

  • Tied to the slot. Group 1 votes with the block. The aggregates must reach the next proposer, so their burst stays near the slot end, with the PTC votes at 9 s.
  • One large aggregate burst. 1,024 aggregates of 663 B arrive together.
  • Uneven load. No FFG traffic from 7 s to 9 s, then the aggregate burst.
  • One failure costs a full slot. A missed aggregate loses the votes of the whole committee in that subnet.

3. Subrounds

Each subnet has 4 committees per slot, of 625 voters each. Each committee votes at the start of its 3 s subround. In the next subround, its aggregators publish while the next committee votes. At any time, one committee votes and one committee aggregates.

Good

  • Decoupled from the block. The pipeline repeats every 3 s and does not use the block. You can shift the grid to keep FFG traffic out of the block window.
  • Even load. No idle time and no single large aggregate burst.
  • Smaller aggregates. A 625-voter aggregate is 429 B, against 663 B.
  • Failure isolation. A missed aggregate loses one quarter of the slot's votes.
  • Free length. A subround does not have to divide the slot. See free-running committees.

Bad

  • More aggregate bytes. Each extra aggregation point adds 1,024 aggregates per slot: 1.8 MB per node.
  • Shorter collection time. Every vote has 3 s to reach the aggregators.
  • Bigger vote bursts. 625 voters per subnet publish together, against 357.
  • Inclusion delay changes with the committee. Aggregates finish at different times in the slot.

4. One mechanism, two settings

Give staggered publishing a second aggregation point at 5 s. The 5 s aggregate holds groups 1–4. The 9 s aggregate holds groups 5–7. Each set of groups now has its own aggregate, so each set is a committee. This is the subround structure with 2 committees.

So both systems are points in one design space with two settings:

  • Vote schedule: when each voter publishes. Staggered: 7 times per slot. Subrounds: 4 times.
  • Aggregation points: when aggregators publish, and which votes each aggregate holds. Staggered: 1 per slot. Subrounds: 4.

The vote bytes do not change with the settings. The aggregate bytes grow with the number of aggregation points n. The bitlist bytes per slot stay about the same, but the fixed 350 B overhead is paid n times.

5. Average FFG load

This view spreads each burst evenly over its window. It shows the steady load, but it hides the bursts. Votes publish within about 200 ms of their scheduled time, and aggregators publish at one instant. The next section models this.

6. Bursts: contention with the block and Goldfish votes

The model: each publication arrives at a node as a box. All of its bytes arrive evenly within 0.5 s (publish jitter plus propagation). The block arrives from 70 ms, the payload and columns from 190 ms, the Goldfish votes from 150 ms. FFG votes and aggregates arrive at their scheduled times. The slot repeats, so traffic from the end of one slot wraps into the next.

  • Staggered. The slot start carries 48 Mbit/s: block data 26, Goldfish 8, group 1 votes 14. The peak is the aggregate burst at 9 s: 54 Mbit/s.
  • Subrounds, aligned. Committee 1's votes (25) and committee 4's aggregates (35) land on the block data and the Goldfish votes: 94 Mbit/s.
  • Subrounds, shifted. FFG traffic does not touch the block window, and votes and aggregates do not overlap. The peak is one aggregate burst: 35 Mbit/s.

The burst width sets the height of the peaks. With 1 s boxes, the peaks are 27, 47 and 35 Mbit/s. The order stays the same. More blobs make the block window heavier: with 14 blobs, the staggered slot start is 63 Mbit/s. This is one more reason to keep FFG traffic out of it.

8. Other possibilities

  • Shift the subround grid. A shift of about 1 s keeps FFG traffic out of the block and Goldfish window. The shift costs nothing: the pipeline does not use the block.
  • Separate aggregates from votes. Publish the aggregates of committee k−1 about 1.5 s after committee k votes. The two largest FFG bursts then never overlap. The aggregate window grows from 3 s to 4.5 s.
  • Spread the votes in a subround. Two vote groups, 1.5 s apart, halve the vote burst. Three groups 1 s apart always put one group in the block window.
  • Start staggered groups at 1 s. The slot start falls from 48 to 34 Mbit/s. The aggregate burst at 9 s stays at 54 Mbit/s.
  • Spread the aggregator publications. The 16 aggregators of a committee do not have to publish at the same instant. A wider publish window lowers the aggregate burst in both systems.
  • Use fewer subrounds. 2 or 3 subrounds cut the aggregate overhead (section 4) and keep the free phase. 3 subrounds of 4 s match the free-running committee clock.

9. What the model leaves out

  • Box shape. Real arrivals have tails. The peaks scale about with 1 / burst width.
  • IHAVE and IWANT. Control traffic for FFG topics can be large. It is not in the model.
  • IDONTWANT. It removes some duplicates of large messages, such as the payload. A factor of 5 can be high for those.
  • PTC votes. They arrive at 9 s and add to the staggered aggregate burst. They are not in the model.
  • Blobs. The model uses 3 blobs. Each blob adds about 2.1 KB to each custody column.
  • Upload. The model assumes that upload equals download.
