There’s now a push to try PQ Gasper first. On ACDC, Justin Traglia suggested a few weeks on PQ Gasper, since most of that work is needed for PQ-DC too; Ben framed PQ Gasper as the quick-deploy fallback and PQ-DC as the primary path. Either way it affects us, and it’s an opportunity: networking we ship for PQ Gasper, PQ-DC can reuse. As long as it’s an upgrade, not a shortcut.

Three buckets. One: upgrades PQ Gasper would need that we’d keep for PQ-DC. Two: shortcuts that make PQ Gasper work but that we’d have to revert for PQ-DC. Three: greenfield PQ-DC. With BLS the rule was to change little; that constraint is gone now, which opens up new designs. Today: the first two. Greenfield PQ-DC in later calls, depending on how things go.

Starting point. From our Shadow runs and Anton’s jvm-libp2p simulations, it doesn’t seem we can disseminate ~1,600 PQ attestations per subnet in ~4 seconds if we keep everything else equal. That’s what 16 subnets with 800k validators need. leanXMSS signatures are ~2.4 KB, 25× a BLS signature, parameters not final. [click] And PQ-DC with 8-slot rounds carries 4× the votes per slot of a 32-slot epoch.

No leanVM: ~16k validator cap, or > 10 h to finality

The avenues from my write-up, sorted as a first cut; question marks are where I’m least sure which side they fall on. I’m not saying any of these are good, the point is to understand the trade-offs. Keep: a new vote and payload broadcast, e.g. on waggle with partial messages, EC broadcast and per-topic priority; a higher EIP-7870 bandwidth floor that scales with validators per node; a validator cap or more consolidation; solo stakers joining subnets only for aggregator duty. Revert: votes sent point-to-point to ~128 aggregators, no vote privacy; builders aggregating; 16 aggregators down to 4; a slower FFG cadence; 24-second slots. Without leanVM we get ~500 signatures per block, so either a ~16k validator cap or more than 10 hours to finality at 1M validators.
