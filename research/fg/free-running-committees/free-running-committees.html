Free-running FG committees

One committee every 4 seconds, and rounds of 23 units that are allowed to slide across slot boundaries. Committees then rotate through the slot on their own, every validator votes exactly every 92 s, validators can pick their own seat, and the round becomes pure accounting. The appendix asks where each FG design picks its target and what that does to time-to-finality.

Provenance: agent. Written on 2026-10-06 by an agent from Yann's dictated ideas: free-running committees, pick-your-seat and the sticky-target concern. TL;DR

  1. The unit (sub-round design). A committee starts every 4 s; it votes for 4 s (push only) and then aggregates for 4 s. Once per slot, a 2 s IHAVE/IWANT window opens at +8 s, followed by a 2 s final aggregation at +10 s, so the next proposer gets the best aggregate. This requires Q18's vote windows to stay open until about +10.5 s.
  2. Francesco's (index + round) mod C, on the DC draft's slot-aligned rounds, moves each cohort one unit later per round. Gaps are 100 s, and once per round the cohort that wraps around votes twice within 8 s. That is a one-cohort version of the overlap Mikhail wanted to avoid.
  3. Proposal: a free-running committee clock. Committee = unit mod 23. A round is 23 units, 92 s, "eight slots minus one committee", not tied to slots.
    • Every validator votes exactly every 92 s, and every block carries three committees.
    • In-slot positions rotate on their own (+0 → +8 → +4 s). No position function and no position seed is needed. The rotation is ε-fair in 13 min, provided the round boundary itself carries no penalty.
    • Composition still needs Q18's era hash, or capped seat choice.
    • Drawn on the slot grid, it is a rotation one unit earlier per 8 slots, with Q17's idle unit filled.
    • Its latency gain is small and depends on the model: −8 s per height at p = 1 in the explicit-time model, none at 2/3. The case is structural, not latency. It costs +4.3 % FG traffic.
  4. Pick-your-seat. On the free-running clock every seat has the same in-slot timing, so choosing a seat only chooses who you vote with.
    • It is acceptable for load and liveness only with a stake cap and a count cap on every inflow.
    • Uncapped, an attacker can pack 8× a seat's load into one unit and burst 67 MB.
    • Capped seats can still drift, over months, towards whole seats owned by one operator or an adversary. Choice replaces the era re-draw's periodic reset with slowness.
  5. Extra: where targets come from. With heights advancing at 2/3, sticky voters, who keep the old target for reward fairness, lock heights to rounds.
    • That costs +50 s of expected lag at p = 1 (174 → 224 s), and becomes a cliff near 2/3: +111 s at p = 0.8, +220 s at 0.7.
    • Previous-target credit makes rewards fair without stickiness.
    • If round-aligned voting is required, pick the target at the round start; prefix counting is faster (184 s) but its finalization is untested.
    • Rolling FFG is fastest and flat (168 s).

1The unit: a committee every 4 seconds

Q17 fixed the shape of a unit, the voting slot of one cohort. A cohort votes for 4 s; then its aggregators publish and the aggregates spread for another 4 s, exactly like today's attestation pipeline. A new unit starts every 4 s, so three cohorts start in every slot, at +0, +4 and +8 s. Terminology: this page calls the group that votes in a unit a committee, which is Q18's cohort or seat. On the wire each one splits into 64 subnet committees (Q18 §7).

Two additions make this the sub-round design:

  • Push only inside the phases. Votes and on-time aggregates travel by eager push, and FG topics emit no IHAVE gossip while a phase runs. Anton's traffic simulations put almost all GossipSub control traffic on IHAVEs for FG votes and aggregates: about 30 % of a home node's traffic, and up to 250 Mbit/s of IHAVEs on a supernode (FF chat, 2026-10-01/02).
  • One repair window per slot. At +8 s, four seconds before the slot ends, nodes run a single 2 s IHAVE/IWANT round for the FG messages of the three committees the next block will carry. At +10 s a few late aggregators publish the best aggregate they now hold, a superset of the on-time one, and it reaches the next proposer by +12 s. This is Q18's late aggregator, and Anton's request for a few very late aggregators (FF chat, 2026-09-30), moved onto a fixed, slot-synchronous grid.

So, on the free-running clock of §3, the block at the end of every slot carries exactly three committees: those that started at −4, +0 and +4 s. A vote cast at time t lands in the first block at or after t + 8 s.

What the repair window changes elsewhere

  • Vote windows. Under Q18's rules, the −4 s and +0 s committees' votes are already ignored at +8 s: the soft end is the unit start + 8.5 s. The repair window therefore extends the soft end to about +10.5 s. Then four windows are open at +8…+10 s instead of three, and Q18's burst bound grows from 3β to 4β units: 11.6 MB instead of 8.7 MB at β = 1/3 and 10⁶ validators (§4 has the seat-choice version).
  • Gossip. "No IHAVE during the phases" needs per-topic IHAVE scheduling in the clients, a GossipSub change that sits outside Q17's "only the round architecture is tuned". Anton's caveat applies: deferring IHAVE mostly saves duplicate IWANTs, not IHAVE volume. The IHAVE saving needs batching, or not advertising what push already delivered (IDONTWANT, partial messages).
  • Best effort. The 2 s final hop is shorter than Q17's 4 s propagation budget, so it is a best-effort improvement. The on-time aggregate stays the guaranteed path.

2Francesco's rotation, on the slot grid

Francesco's point (FF chat, 2026-10-01) was that committees are a load unit, not a security unit. So they need no shuffle, only a fair deterministic map from (round, index) to committee. His simplest version is "index + round % number of committees", with the index taken in the active set.

Read on the DC draft's slot-aligned 8-slot rounds and Q17's grid (23 units, the 24th left idle), the formula moves every cohort one unit later per round:

  • Most cohorts wait 25 units, 100 s, between votes.
  • Once per round, the cohort that wraps from the last seat to the first votes at the end of one round and again at the start of the next, 8 s later. This is a one-cohort (1/23 of stake) version of the overlap Mikhail wanted to avoid. His example was the last quarter of a round voting again at the start of the next.
  • The in-slot offset changes every round, so in-slot fairness comes quickly (0.38 h).

Q18 measured this rotation at 0.727 rounds per height, against 0.696 for a fixed order. It recommended two changes: use the raw validator index, because active-set ranks re-seat everyone behind each exit, and step one seat every 8 rounds instead of every round. The first still applies. The proposal below replaces the second, unless the round boundary itself carries a penalty (§3).

3The proposal: let the clock run free

Keep the 4 s unit grid, but stop forcing rounds to start on a slot boundary. Number units from genesis, u = ⌊t / 4 s⌋, and let committee k = u mod 23 vote in unit u. A round is then just 23 consecutive units: 92 s, or eight slots minus one committee. The unit that Q17 left idle becomes the first committee of the next round.

Definition (C = 23 committees, three 4 s units per 12 s slot)

active_committee(u) = u mod C          # the only duty function
round(u)            = u // C           # accounting only
unit_start(u)       = genesis + 4·u s  # slot u // 3, offset 4·(u mod 3) s
round_start(r)      = unit_start(C·r)  # +0, +8, +4 s into its slot
seat(v)             ∈ [0, C)           # hashed, striped or chosen (§4)
v votes in unit u   ⇔ u mod C == seat(v)

What follows from it:

  • Every validator votes exactly every 92 s, unless its seat changes (an era re-draw or a seat request). The gap is always C units, the optimum in Q18's gap lemma. No cohort waits C + 1 or 2C − 1 units, and none votes twice in a row, so the end-of-round overlap is gone by construction.
  • The rotation is free. 23 is not a multiple of 3, so each committee's in-slot offset steps +0 → +8 → +4 s, one step per round, and repeats every 3 rounds (276 s, or 23 slots). Position needs no rotation function, no seed and no period K, and in-slot effects even out within 13 min.
    • Caveat: this covers in-slot effects only. Seats are fixed in the round, so if the round boundary itself carried a penalty, the free-running clock would never even it out: 0.93 pp for one cohort under Q18's assumed rates. Penalties of that kind would be a round-start freeze, or a first-unit miss rate. Q18's slow rotation on top (3.3 h) would then be the fix.
    • Composition, meaning who shares a cohort, is a separate axis. It still needs Q18's era hash, or capped seat choice (§4).
  • Every block carries three committees. In Q17, the first slot of each round landed only two aggregates (its "Problem 1"). Here every slot is identical.
  • The round boundary drifts 4 s earlier for every 96 s of slot time. Two rounds out of three start mid-slot. The round stays in the protocol as accounting: rewards, the vote's round field, bit layout and the inclusion window. Nothing has to happen when a round starts.
  • Cost: no unit is idle, so a validator votes every 92 s instead of every 96 s. FG traffic per second rises by 24/23 (+4.3 %) at the same per-unit load, and the 4 s breather Q17 had once per round is gone.

Reading the latency columns: do not sell this on latency.

  • At the 2/3 threshold every schedule takes 108 s, because the block grid absorbs the 4 s. A height ends with the block that carries its last needed committee, at 12·⌈(G + 2)/3⌉ s, which is 108 s for G = 23, 24 and 25 alike [derived].
  • The −8 s at p = 1 depends on the model. It disappears if the +0 s committee can see its slot's block before signing: both clocks then take 72 s, and both take 96 s at the threshold. A free-running clock with C = 24, which adds no traffic, also reaches 72 s at p = 1.
  • The −4 s at p = 0.8 comes from random offline sets. With offline stake spread evenly, both clocks give 96 s.

The wins that do not depend on modelling are structural: a constant gap, no double vote, identical blocks, and in-slot rotation for free. A new minor cost is that 8.7 % of blocks carry votes from two rounds, which cannot share one aggregate.

Is this what Francesco meant?

Not as written. The chat does not say who first suggested letting rounds drift. Francesco's formula, read with the DC draft's slot-aligned rounds, is the forward +1 rotation of §2. The precise relation is this:

  • On the slot-aligned grid (Figure 2, slot view), the free-running clock is also a rotation. Position j of 96 s window R holds cohort (j + R) mod 23. Every cohort moves one unit earlier per 96 s, and all 24 positions are used.
  • Q18 rejected the backward direction ("−1 unit every round", 2.0 rounds per height near 2/3) because inside fixed 23-unit rounds the wrapping cohort waits 2C − 1 units. Filling the idle unit removes the wrap: the cohort that would be pushed out of the round votes as the next round's first committee.

So the difference is this. Francesco's formula rotates the cohorts through the round. The free-running clock keeps the cohort order fixed and rotates the round through the slots. Adding (index + round) mod C on top of the free drift (schedule e above) only costs: the worst gap stretches to C + 1, a double vote 4 s apart returns, and in-slot fairness slows to 4.9 h because 24 ≡ 0 (mod 3). If round-position penalties exist, the right addition is Q18's slow rotation (3.3 h), not the per-round step.

What changes in the spec

  • Round ≠ 8 slots. Define compute_round_at_unit, compute_round_at_slot (the round of the slot's first unit) and the round's start slot and offset. The vote keeps its round field: seat, bit layout and inclusion window are functions of it (Q18 §10).
  • Inclusion. "Current or previous round" now counts in units. The block of slot s carries units 3s − 4 … 3s − 2, which can straddle a round boundary.
  • Epochs and eras. A 32-slot epoch holds 96/23 ≈ 4.17 rounds, and 22.9 % of rounds straddle an epoch boundary. Q18's index set Active(epoch(round)) uses the epoch of the round's first unit. If Q18's era hash stays, era boundaries must snap to rounds: 256 epochs is 1,068.5 rounds. 1,056 rounds (253 epochs) keeps Q18's zero-slack invariant, era ≤ 256 epochs.
  • C must be coprime with the units per slot. With 3 units per slot that means C ∈ {22, 23, 25, 26, …}; with 10 s quick slots and 2 units per slot, C must be odd. C = 24 would freeze every committee's in-slot offset forever.
  • Round position must stay neutral. Under Mikhail's instant switch nothing waits for a round to start, because heights advance inside blocks. Two asymmetries remain. First, the inclusion window ("current or previous round") gives the round's last seat 88 s to be included and its first seat 176 s; both are ample, since votes land within 8–16 s. Second, a design that picks targets at round starts (§5) picks them mid-slot two rounds out of three, as "the head at the round's first unit". Adding any round-start freeze would bring back a first-unit penalty, and the free-running clock would not even it out.

4Pick your seat

Once the active committee index alone decides when a seat votes, a seat can be any public number in [0, 23). Here a seat is Q18's cohort: the group that votes in one unit. On the wire it splits into 64 subnet committees. Seat choice could work like this:

  • Default seat. Q18's era hash, or the seed-free stripe v mod 23.
  • Choice. A validator may request a seat through an fg_seat field, set by an EL-triggered request (like EIP-7002 exits and EIP-7251 consolidations). The request is churn-limited and takes effect at an era boundary (or any round boundary at least 5 epochs ahead).
  • Caps. A request is refused or queued if it would push a seat above κ · S / 23 of stake (κ ≈ 1.05) or above a validator-count cap. The stake cap alone bounds message load only when validator sizes are similar: a seat of 32 ETH validators carries 64× the messages of the same stake in 2048 ETH validators.
  • Subnets within a seat. They must be specified too: a stripe of the validator index (no seed) or Q18's era hash (keeps the seed). An operator gets local aggregation only if it fills a whole seat, about 4.3 % of stake, or also chooses its subnets.
  • Membership. Gossip and STF membership stay a pure function of the seat and Active(epoch(round)), with no shuffle.

Why the free-running clock makes this reasonable: every seat has the same 92 s gap and passes through all three in-slot offsets every 3 rounds, so no seat is better for in-slot timing. A choice is therefore about who you vote with, as in Yann's "a committee of only Binance validators" question. Relative-order effects remain: at p = 0.8 two thirds of one cohort's votes can go stale (below), and previous-target credit has to neutralise that.

What can go wrong, and why the caps must cover every inflow

From results/clock.md §7, at 10⁶ validators [sim, calc]. Seat composition is not an FG security unit (Q18 §8), so none of this touches the height FG's safety. It is about load, liveness and partial tallies. Capped figures use κ = 1.1, which is conservative against the proposed 1.05.

  • Load. Without caps:
    • operators co-locating put 2.2–4.2× a seat's normal load into one unit;
    • a herd or an attacker puts 7.6–8.3×, up to 72.5 MB in one 4 s unit;
    • heights at p = 1 slow from 72 s to 79–92 s.
    A stake cap plus a count cap keeps every seat near κ.
  • Bursts. Hashed seats keep Q18's vote windows at 3β units (8.7 MB at β = 1/3), or 4β (11.6 MB) once §1's repair window extends the soft end.
    • An adversary that owns whole consecutive seats can release min(βC, 3κ) units at once: 26–29 MB. With the repair window, min(βC, 4κ) units: 35–38 MB, three times the hashed bound.
    • Uncapped, the burst is βC = 7.67 units (66.7 MB), as if there were no windows.
    • If honest validators already fill the seats, the cap leaves the adversary ≈ 1.3 units.
  • Honest minorities. In a mixed seat the majority chooser holds most aggregator slots.
    • Uncapped, an attacker with β = 1/3 can dilute a seat so that 27 % of its committees have no honest aggregator. With the cap the probability is ≤ 5.5·10⁻⁵.
    • A seat filled entirely by one operator harms nobody, though it deanonymises that operator.
    • One filled to 95 % leaves 45 % of its committees without an aggregator from the 5 % minority.
  • Partial tallies. At β = 1/3, the adversary's largest share of any 4 consecutive units is 33.6 % with default striped seats, 39 % with the cap, 74 % uncapped and 100 % with whole seats. This matters only to a gadget that reads partial tallies (an SG, a fast confirmation).
  • Capture speed. Going from 10 % of stake to 1/3 of one seat takes:
    • 9.5 days uncapped (churn-limited);
    • 118 days (Electra churn) or 30 days (EIP-8061) with κ = 1.1;
    • for comparison, 8.3 or 4.4 days under Q18's fixed striping.
    The capped numbers assume honest validators never move, which is exactly what seat choice invites. Q18's era re-draw instead resets every 27 h. Every inflow needs the caps, including consolidation targets and top-ups.
  • Withholding is not worse. An adversary that owns whole consecutive seats and stays silent costs 92 s per height at β = 1/3, against 108 s when its stake is spread. Its dead arc absorbs the post-switch lag.
  • Relative order. The order of seats never changes, so in round-locked regimes the same cohorts cast the stale votes: up to 67 % of one cohort's votes at p = 0.8. A rotation cannot spread this on the free-running clock. Previous-target credit (mk:124-134) keeps it reward-neutral.

The trade against Q18's era hash.

  • With a striped default and capped choice, the protocol needs no seed, no era-boundary delay and no era invariant.
  • It loses the periodic reset: composition becomes slow to capture rather than re-drawn.
  • Choice is strictly more power than grinding ever gave.

What it needs:

  • a 1-byte seat field (1 MB at 10⁶ validators);
  • per-seat stake and count totals;
  • a ~69-byte EIP-7685 request;
  • both cap checks on every inflow;
  • a subnet rule within the seat.

It is compatible with Active(epoch(round)) if a seat change takes effect at epoch ≥ c + 5 and on a round boundary. A state-held seat can differ across forks. That is fine for the height FG, but not for a continuous FG whose slashing reads a validator's tick.

Verdict. Pick-your-seat is acceptable for load and liveness with a stake cap and a count cap on every inflow; FG safety is not the question. Even capped, seats drift over months towards whole seats held by one operator or an adversary. Keep Q18's era hash as the default, and revisit seat choice before any SG or fast-confirmation rule reads partial tallies.

5Extra: where targets come from, and what it costs

The question (Yann, from a discussion with Mikhail on 2026-10-05). Suppose a height advances as soon as 2/3 have voted, and there is no prefix consensus or Rolling FFG. Then the next height's target is fixed at that instant: it is the block that completed the quorum, the height's entry. Under Mikhail's instant switch, the rest of the round votes for it at once. Yann's team favours keeping the old target for the rest of the round, for reward fairness. If voters do that, the entry is first voted on in the next round. By then it is already old (a third of a round in the ideal model), and latency grows.

The five designs below run on the same free-running clock with the unit timing of §1. They differ only in where the target comes from and when voters switch.

designtarget of the next checkpointvoters switch
Floating heights, instant switch (Mikhail)the block that completes the 2/3 quorum (entry)at once; previous-target votes keep reward credit
Floating heights, sticky votersthe same entryonly at their next round: the round's votes all name one height
Round-aligned target (control)the head when the round startsat round starts
Prefix counting, sticky votersnone fixed: votes name their head, and the round justifies the deepest block that 2/3 of its votes extendat round starts
Rolling FFGevery unit is a checkpoint (a δ-deep block)no heights to switch

Latency is reported, as in the continuous-FG note, as an expected value over block birth time: E[lag] = E[time to inclusion] + (inclusion → justified) + (justified → finalized). Best and worst are the minimum and maximum over birth time. "Time to inclusion" is the wait until the gadget adopts the block (or a descendant) as a target.

  1. Sticky voters give back what floating heights buy. Heights lock to one per round, and E[lag] rises from 174 s to 224 s at p = 1 (+50 s). In the ideal model it goes from 5/3 to 5/2 rounds, exactly DC today's terms. Stale votes rise from 11 % to 26 % of honest votes.
  2. Near 2/3 it is a cliff. Once the quorum block lands after the next round's first unit, that round re-votes a closed height: 345 s at p = 0.8 (+111 s) and 454 s at p = 0.7 (+220 s). Voters that source from the confirmed block see the quorum about 17 s later, so they fall off already at p = 1 (283 s). A minority suffices: 20 % sticky stake locks heights to rounds at p = 1.
  3. "Picked deep" is mild at full participation and real near 2/3. At p = 1 the entry is only 20 s old at its first sticky vote, because the 8 s aggregation already puts the quorum 68–76 s into the 92 s round. At p = 0.8 it is 48 s old, at 0.7 88 s. Even at p = 1 the cost is the whole round between pick and justification (92 s instead of 72 s), paid again before finality. What a design does with its target matters more than the target's age at the pick.
  4. If round-aligned voting is wanted, pick the target at the round start, or count by prefix.
    • Round-aligned targets cost 212 s at p = 1 but have no cliff at 0.8 (232 s).
    • Prefix counting with sticky voters stays within +10 s of the instant switch at p = 1 (184 s), and is slightly faster at 0.9–0.8 (201 and 225 s). At p = 1 its justified block keeps deepening for 20 s after the first quorum (16 s and 12 s at 0.9 and 0.8).
    • Both break at p = 0.7 with k = 1 finalization: 324 s mean, max 456 s. When voters source from the confirmed block, they never finalize at all.
    • A Gasper-style variant (source pinned at the round start, k ≤ 2) was simulated for the round-aligned target only. It holds p = 0.7 to 328 s mean (max 372 s; 344 s with confirmed-block sources) and costs +29 s at p = 0.8. For prefix counting it is untested.
  5. Rolling FFG is fastest at every participation level, with a flat lag and no stale votes: 168 / 192 / 204 / 216 s at δ = 1, 12 s less at δ = 0. The ideal 20 % gain over floating heights shrinks to 3–13 %, because it pays the 8 s aggregation in both phases.
Recommendation. Keep the instant switch, and get reward fairness from previous-target credit (Q18 §3), which already pays stale votes. Stickiness is the most expensive way to make rewards fair. On the free-running clock this is also the natural fit, since nothing has to happen when a round starts. If the team still wants round-aligned voting, take the round-aligned target with Gasper-style k ≤ 2: it is simulated, and has no cliff. Prefix counting is faster, but its round-aligned finalization breaks at p = 0.7, and its k ≤ 2 variant and its safety are untested.

Not modelled: AC forks and entry splits, timeouts and the R1–R3 races, missed slots. For those adversarial worst cases see the prefix-consensus study (Rolling FFG is immune to R1–R3). The prefix design's k = 1 finalization is a timing model; its safety was not checked.

6Method, numbers and open questions

  • Clock lane: sim/clock.py (python3 sim/clock.py all, ~6 s), write-up in results/clock.md.
    • It reuses Q18's simulator for the constant-lag model and reproduces Q18's 0.696 / 0.826 / 1.130 rounds per height exactly.
    • It adds an explicit-time model of §1: a vote cast at t lands in the block at 12·⌈(t + 8)/12⌉ s, and the quorum block becomes visible 1 s after its slot start. The model uses 40·C validators and Q18's random offline sets.
  • Design lane: sim/designs.py (all, ~70 s; traces writes Figure 5's data), write-up in results/designs.md.
    • It is a copy of the prefix-consensus simulator with 92 s rounds and three new engines: sticky voters, round-aligned target, and prefix with sticky voters.
    • At C = 24 it reproduces the prefix study's tables byte for byte. Its ideal-model runs match the closed forms 5/3, 5/2, 13/6, 11/6 and 4/3.
  • Independent check: sim/check_frc.py, about 80 lines by the orchestrating agent. It spreads offline stake evenly over cohorts and reproduces:
    • the vote gaps and the committees per block;
    • the explicit-time seconds per height: 72 / 84 / 96 / 96 / 108 s for the free-running clock at p = 1 / 0.9 / 0.8 / 0.7 / 0.67.
    It agrees with the clock lane at p = 1, 0.9 and 0.67. At 0.8 and 0.7 the clock lane's random offline sets average shorter and longer heights: 92 and 98.2 s.
  • This page: python3 build.py inlines src/ and results/*.json into one file. Plain JavaScript, no libraries.

Open questions

  1. The +8 s pull window, on the devnet. What share of votes is still missing at +8 s, per unit offset? Does one 2 s IHAVE/IWANT round recover them? Does per-topic IHAVE scheduling cut IHAVE volume, or only duplicate IWANTs (Anton's caveat)?
  2. When can the +0 s committee sign? If it can see its slot's block first, the threshold latency drops from 108 s to 96 s on both clocks.
  3. Rewards. The choice between the instant switch with previous-target credit and sticky voting (§5).
  4. Pick-your-seat. Whether to allow it before an SG or fast-confirmation rule is specified, the cap κ, and how seat requests interact with consolidations and top-ups.
  5. 10 s quick slots (CFI'd for H*). The grid has to be redrawn. With 2 units per slot, C must be odd (e.g. 15).
