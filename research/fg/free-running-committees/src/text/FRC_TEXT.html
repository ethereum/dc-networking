Keep the 4 s unit grid, but stop forcing rounds to start on a slot boundary. Number units from genesis, u = ⌊t / 4 s⌋, and let committee k = u mod 23 vote in unit u. A round is then just 23 consecutive units: 92 s, or eight slots minus one committee. The unit that Q17 left idle becomes the first committee of the next round.

Definition (C = 23 committees, three 4 s units per 12 s slot)

active_committee(u) = u mod C          # the only duty function
round(u)            = u // C           # accounting only
unit_start(u)       = genesis + 4·u s  # slot u // 3, offset 4·(u mod 3) s
round_start(r)      = unit_start(C·r)  # +0, +8, +4 s into its slot
seat(v)             ∈ [0, C)           # hashed, striped or chosen (§4)
v votes in unit u   ⇔ u mod C == seat(v)

What follows from it:

  • Every validator votes exactly every 92 s, unless its seat changes (an era re-draw or a seat request). The gap is always C units, the optimum in Q18's gap lemma. No cohort waits C + 1 or 2C − 1 units, and none votes twice in a row, so the end-of-round overlap is gone by construction.
  • The rotation is free. 23 is not a multiple of 3, so each committee's in-slot offset steps +0 → +8 → +4 s, one step per round, and repeats every 3 rounds (276 s, or 23 slots). Position needs no rotation function, no seed and no period K, and in-slot effects even out within 13 min.
    • Caveat: this covers in-slot effects only. Seats are fixed in the round, so if the round boundary itself carried a penalty, the free-running clock would never even it out: 0.93 pp for one cohort under Q18's assumed rates. Penalties of that kind would be a round-start freeze, or a first-unit miss rate. Q18's slow rotation on top (3.3 h) would then be the fix.
    • Composition, meaning who shares a cohort, is a separate axis. It still needs Q18's era hash, or capped seat choice (§4).
  • Every block carries three committees. In Q17, the first slot of each round landed only two aggregates (its "Problem 1"). Here every slot is identical.
  • The round boundary drifts 4 s earlier for every 96 s of slot time. Two rounds out of three start mid-slot. The round stays in the protocol as accounting: rewards, the vote's round field, bit layout and the inclusion window. Nothing has to happen when a round starts.
  • Cost: no unit is idle, so a validator votes every 92 s instead of every 96 s. FG traffic per second rises by 24/23 (+4.3 %) at the same per-unit load, and the 4 s breather Q17 had once per round is gone.

Reading the latency columns: do not sell this on latency.

  • At the 2/3 threshold every schedule takes 108 s, because the block grid absorbs the 4 s. A height ends with the block that carries its last needed committee, at 12·⌈(G + 2)/3⌉ s, which is 108 s for G = 23, 24 and 25 alike [derived].
  • The −8 s at p = 1 depends on the model. It disappears if the +0 s committee can see its slot's block before signing: both clocks then take 72 s, and both take 96 s at the threshold. A free-running clock with C = 24, which adds no traffic, also reaches 72 s at p = 1.
  • The −4 s at p = 0.8 comes from random offline sets. With offline stake spread evenly, both clocks give 96 s.

The wins that do not depend on modelling are structural: a constant gap, no double vote, identical blocks, and in-slot rotation for free. A new minor cost is that 8.7 % of blocks carry votes from two rounds, which cannot share one aggregate.

Is this what Francesco meant?

Not as written. The chat does not say who first suggested letting rounds drift. Francesco's formula, read with the DC draft's slot-aligned rounds, is the forward +1 rotation of §2. The precise relation is this:

  • On the slot-aligned grid (Figure 2, slot view), the free-running clock is also a rotation. Position j of 96 s window R holds cohort (j + R) mod 23. Every cohort moves one unit earlier per 96 s, and all 24 positions are used.
  • Q18 rejected the backward direction ("−1 unit every round", 2.0 rounds per height near 2/3) because inside fixed 23-unit rounds the wrapping cohort waits 2C − 1 units. Filling the idle unit removes the wrap: the cohort that would be pushed out of the round votes as the next round's first committee.

So the difference is this. Francesco's formula rotates the cohorts through the round. The free-running clock keeps the cohort order fixed and rotates the round through the slots. Adding (index + round) mod C on top of the free drift (schedule e above) only costs: the worst gap stretches to C + 1, a double vote 4 s apart returns, and in-slot fairness slows to 4.9 h because 24 ≡ 0 (mod 3). If round-position penalties exist, the right addition is Q18's slow rotation (3.3 h), not the per-round step.

What changes in the spec

  • Round ≠ 8 slots. Define compute_round_at_unit, compute_round_at_slot (the round of the slot's first unit) and the round's start slot and offset. The vote keeps its round field: seat, bit layout and inclusion window are functions of it (Q18 §10).
  • Inclusion. "Current or previous round" now counts in units. The block of slot s carries units 3s − 4 … 3s − 2, which can straddle a round boundary.
  • Epochs and eras. A 32-slot epoch holds 96/23 ≈ 4.17 rounds, and 22.9 % of rounds straddle an epoch boundary. Q18's index set Active(epoch(round)) uses the epoch of the round's first unit. If Q18's era hash stays, era boundaries must snap to rounds: 256 epochs is 1,068.5 rounds. 1,056 rounds (253 epochs) keeps Q18's zero-slack invariant, era ≤ 256 epochs.
  • C must be coprime with the units per slot. With 3 units per slot that means C ∈ {22, 23, 25, 26, …}; with 10 s quick slots and 2 units per slot, C must be odd. C = 24 would freeze every committee's in-slot offset forever.
  • Round position must stay neutral. Under Mikhail's instant switch nothing waits for a round to start, because heights advance inside blocks. Two asymmetries remain. First, the inclusion window ("current or previous round") gives the round's last seat 88 s to be included and its first seat 176 s; both are ample, since votes land within 8–16 s. Second, a design that picks targets at round starts (§5) picks them mid-slot two rounds out of three, as "the head at the round's first unit". Adding any round-start freeze would bring back a first-unit penalty, and the free-running clock would not even it out.
